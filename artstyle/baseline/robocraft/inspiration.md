Robocraft has gone through 3 eras of artstyle:
- 90's looking futuristic shooter with dark and gritty maps and glossy detailed cubes, maps feel like a random location with objects like bridges and barriers placed for no reason other than convenience.
- Plastic-y futuristic shooter with brighter maps and glossy cubes, locations in maps feeling more like captured areas repurposed for a battle for fun.
- futuristic neon fortnite clone shooter with the reworked vibrant maps and cartoony and bloom coated cubes, maps feeling more like actual battle zones with barriers placed as actual cover and bridges being more logical.

During the first era, RC was converted from a single player survival game (originally a physics puzzle game) to fit a multiplayer game.
This has made the maps feel more like quickly made arenas, everything felt dark and the voice-overs really captured that 90's era multiplayer game.
This did cause a problem, the blocks of the bots and weapons were in a completely different style than the maps, making them feel disconnected but allowing you to see the enemies even in the dark and moody settings.
Even the brighter snowy maps had your screen obscured by falling snow, and the fog of war effect to make the game run on even a potato made seeing far into the distance an impossibility.

While this was nice, the disconnect made the artstyle feel rushed, they could have made the bots more rusty or simply removed the texture from the cubes to be more futuristic, but now it felt like a mix in between.
The later Carbon cubes removed this and allowed things to flow more naturally than the overly textured, overly gossy cubes.

The second era came with the mega weapons and bulky weapons (flak/loml/ion) as well as the cube reworks. Reworking the game to feel less 90's and more futuristic.
Maps were made with more detail and more light, stylization was removed in favour of more crisp and square menus with less animation.
The problem with this that it felt like the stylization was removed with nothing added back for it, an experiment into the era of colouring your bot rather than an upgrading vehicle of war shooter.

While the more connected artstyle of the second era was nice, the problem with it was that there was no theme to it, it felt bland and corporate, or at least experimental with no proper conclusion.
Removing the hood of a car is cool for what it shows but it ends up serving no good purpose.

The last era introduced better maps that had actual design into them, as well as a recreation of all old maps.
All of them felt fantastic, putting the 2nd era Birmingham Power Station to shame with the rework of goddamn Hellion Crater.
Ice maps felt more thought out, more challenging and bright, while still sticking to a now more cartoony tone and design.

This of course also came at a cost, more cosmetics were pointlessly added that went completely out of the scope of what the game was trying to stylize.
While the maps and bots now sat in the same chair, the game design was thrown out the door with flower ropes, bubble blowers and overdone bloom, really high bloom.
The new music did cover up some of the issues by feeling more energetic than the space themed survival-esque choral metal music, now moving to a moreover rock and chimes vibe, but it still couldn't cover up the high bloom of golden weapons and cardboard parts.
